Career Goals

Where I am today, and where I am headed in game development

My Path to Game Development

For the past three years, I have worked behind the scenes of artificial intelligence: testing large language model outputs, documenting edge cases, and helping engineers improve model quality. That work showed me how much I enjoy finding problems, explaining them clearly, and helping build better solutions. Now I am turning that experience into a career in game development.

I have already designed and built my own browser-based action game in JavaScript, complete with power-ups, scaling difficulty, mobile controls, and online two-player matches. Building it taught me that making a game fun takes the same skills I use every day in QA: careful testing, honest feedback, and constant iteration.

It is an encouraging time to enter the field. Game programming builds on the same core skills as software development, and the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects that overall employment of software developers, quality assurance analysts, and testers will grow 10 percent from 2025 to 2035, much faster than the average for all occupations (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics [BLS], 2026). My background in both QA and development puts me in a strong position to grow with that demand.

My Goals

Short-Term · 1–2 years

Build My Game Portfolio

  • Earn my Bachelor of Technology in Software Engineering from Western Governors University by November 2027.
  • Keep expanding my browser game with new levels, enemies, and polish based on player feedback.
  • Learn a game engine such as Unity (C#) or Godot, and publish small games on itch.io.
Mid-Term · 3–5 years

Break Into the Games Industry

  • Join a game studio as a junior gameplay programmer or game QA tester.
  • Contribute gameplay features, tools, and bug fixes to a shipped title.
  • Take part in game jams and learn from senior developers through code reviews and mentorship.
Long-Term · 5+ years

Create Games Players Love

  • Grow into a senior gameplay engineer who designs polished, well-tested game systems.
  • Lead a project or release my own independent game.
  • Mentor others who are moving into game development from non-traditional backgrounds.
